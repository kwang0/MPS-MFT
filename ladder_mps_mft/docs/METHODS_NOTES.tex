\begin{abstract}
These notes summarize the methods currently implemented in the modular
\texttt{ladder\_mps\_mft} workflow.  The calculation combines finite-system
matrix-product-state (MPS) ground states for an interacting two-leg ladder with
a perturbative transverse mean-field (MF) map, explicit density targeting,
fixed-point and physical-orbit validation, and a zero-temperature variational
energy comparison.  This is an implementation-oriented living document, not a
claim of thermodynamic convergence.  Unless stated otherwise, ``accepted''
means numerically self-consistent at the simulated length and bond dimension.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and notation}

The authoritative implementation is the Julia package in
\path{ladder_mps_mft/src}.  The monolithic scripts at the repository root are
retained for legacy provenance but are not the definition of the current
method.

The simulated object is one open two-leg ladder with \(L\) rungs and \(2L\)
physical sites.  A site is denoted \(a=(i,\ell)\), with rung
\(i=1,\ldots,L\), leg \(\ell\in\{0,1\}\), and spin
\(\sigma\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}\).  The MPS ordering is rung-major,
\((1,0),(1,1),(2,0),(2,1),\ldots\).  The stored density is per physical site,
\begin{equation}
  n = \frac{N}{2L}, \qquad N_{\star}=2L n_{\star}.
\end{equation}
All present MF fields and measured correlators are restricted to their real
parts.

\section{Isolated-ladder Hamiltonian}

The bare ladder Hamiltonian is the extended Hubbard model
\begin{align}
\Hlad={}&-t\sum_{i=1}^{L-1}\sum_{\ell,\sigma}
 \left(c^{\dagger}_{i\ell\sigma}c_{i+1,\ell\sigma}+\hc\right)
 -t_0\sum_{i=1}^{L}\sum_{\sigma}
 \left(c^{\dagger}_{i0\sigma}c_{i1\sigma}+\hc\right) \nonumber\\
&+U\sum_{i,\ell}n_{i\ell\uparrow}n_{i\ell\downarrow}
 +V\sum_{\langle a,b\rangle_{\mathrm{lad}}}n_a n_b .
\label{eq:bare-hamiltonian}
\end{align}
Here \(t\) is the leg hopping, \(t_0\) the rung hopping, and the \(V\) sum
contains nearest-neighbor leg and rung bonds.  Open boundary conditions are
used along the ladder.  The inter-ladder hopping \(\tp\) is not inserted as an
explicit third spatial direction; it enters the effective MF map at second
order through \(\tp^2/|\Ep|\).

\subsection{Pair-binding scale and registry}

The hole pair-binding convention used by the registry is
\begin{equation}
 \Ep^{(h)}=E(N-2,2S_z=0)+E(N,2S_z=0)
             -2E(N-1,2S_z=1).
\label{eq:pair-binding}
\end{equation}
A negative value denotes a bound pair.  The MF denominator is positive,
\begin{equation}
  g=\frac{\tp^2}{|\Ep|}, \qquad p=2g.
\label{eq:g-definition}
\end{equation}
By default the code refuses an unbound or zero \(\Ep\).  It first seeks an
exact registry match in \((L,U,V,t_0,n)\), choosing the largest available bond
dimension and then the smallest recorded relative discrepancy.  Optional
interpolation is linear in the \emph{signed} \(\Ep\), only between bracketing
\(t_0\) values with identical \((L,U,V,n)\).  Extrapolation and interpolation
across a sign change are forbidden.  The selected row, endpoints, weights,
and registry hash are stored with every result.

The inexpensive check \(\tp<|\Ep|\) is necessary but not a complete
weak-coupling test.  Where available, \(\tp\) is also compared with the
isolated-ladder spin and charge gaps defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:diagnostics}.

\section{Transverse mean-field construction}
\label{sec:mean-field}

\subsection{Fields, correlators, and effective Hamiltonian}

At SCF step \(k\), collect the applied fields into
\(x_k=(\alpha,\beta,\nu)_k\):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \(\alpha_{ii'}^{\ell\ell'}\): anomalous singlet-pair field;
  \item \(\beta_{\sigma,ii'}^{\ell\ell'}\): spin-resolved normal exchange
        field, with the on-site entry removed; and
  \item \(\nu_{\sigma,i\ell}\): spin-resolved Hartree field, stored in code as
        \texttt{mu\_cdw} and distinct from the global chemical potential.
\end{itemize}
Only pairs with \(|i-i'|\le r_{\mathrm{range}}\) are retained.  Define the real
correlators
\begin{align}
 F_{i'\ell',i\ell}&=\operatorname{Re}
   \mean{c_{i'\ell'\uparrow}c_{i\ell\downarrow}},\\
 X^{\sigma}_{a,b}&=\operatorname{Re}
   \mean{c^{\dagger}_{a\sigma}c_{b\sigma}},
 \qquad n^{\sigma}_{a}=\mean{n_{a\sigma}}.
\end{align}
The effective single-ladder problem is
\begin{equation}
  \Heff[x_k,\mu_k]=\Hlad-\mu_k N+H_{\mathrm{lin}}[x_k],
\label{eq:effective-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
H_{\mathrm{lin}}={}&
-\sum_{ii'\ell\ell'}\alpha_{ii'}^{\ell\ell'}
 \left(c_{i'\ell'\uparrow}c_{i\ell\downarrow}
       +c^{\dagger}_{i\ell\downarrow}c^{\dagger}_{i'\ell'\uparrow}\right)
\nonumber\\
&+\sum_{ii'\ell\ell'\sigma}\beta_{\sigma,ii'}^{\ell\ell'}
 c^{\dagger}_{i\ell\sigma}c_{i'\ell'\sigma}
 +\sum_{i\ell\sigma}\nu_{\sigma,i\ell}n_{i\ell\sigma}.
\label{eq:linear-fields}
\end{align}

\subsection{Geometry-dependent map}

Solving Eq.~\eqref{eq:effective-hamiltonian} and measuring \(F,X,n\) defines
the raw map
\begin{equation}
  y_k=f(x_k), \qquad x_{k+1}=y_k
\label{eq:raw-map}
\end{equation}
during an unmixed probe.  The geometry labels are
\texttt{cubic\_frustrated}, \texttt{cubic\_unfrustrated}, and
\texttt{square}.  For the same-leg anomalous fields, let
\(\bm F=(F_{i'0,i0},F_{i'1,i1})^{\mathsf T}\).  Their exact implemented map is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{pmatrix}\alpha^{00}_{ii'}\\\alpha^{11}_{ii'}\end{pmatrix}
 =p M_G\bm F,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Geometry \(G\) & \(M_G\) & Cross-leg anomalous output
\\ \midrule
frustrated cubic
& \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
& \((\alpha^{10},\alpha^{01})=2p(F_{i'0,i1},F_{i'1,i0})\)
\\
unfrustrated cubic
& \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&3\\3&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
& \((\alpha^{10},\alpha^{01})=2p(F_{i'1,i0},F_{i'0,i1})\)
\\
square
& \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
& \((\alpha^{10},\alpha^{01})=(0,0)\)
\\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The normal fields \(\beta_\sigma\) use the same integer coefficient pattern
with \(F\) replaced by the corresponding \(X^\sigma\) entries and the normal
operator orientation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:linear-fields}; their diagonal on-site
entries are set to zero.  This component-level map is implemented in
\path{src/MeanField.jl}; changes to it require a simultaneous audit of the
variational constants in Sec.~\ref{sec:variational}.

\subsubsection{Bond-space interpretation of the pair and exchange fields}

For each fixed ordered rung pair \((i,i')\), it is useful to regard
\begin{equation}
 \bm{\alpha}_{ii'}=
 \left(\alpha^{00}_{ii'},\alpha^{11}_{ii'},
       \alpha^{01}_{ii'},\alpha^{10}_{ii'}\right)^{\mathsf T}
\end{equation}
as a four-component vector in leg-label space.  The real exchange field
\(\beta_\sigma\) has the analogous vector for each spin \(\sigma\).  The
transverse kernel acts bond by bond: it mixes these four leg labels but never
changes \((i,i')\).  For either
\(q\in\{\alpha,\beta_\sigma\}\), the same-leg and cross-leg blocks decompose as
\begin{align}
 q^{\mathrm{same},\pm}_{ii'}
 &=\frac{q^{00}_{ii'}\pm q^{11}_{ii'}}{\sqrt{2}},
 &
 q^{\times,\pm}_{ii'}
 &=\frac{q^{01}_{ii'}\pm q^{10}_{ii'}}{\sqrt{2}}.
 \label{eq:bond-leg-modes}
\end{align}
The \(+\) and \(-\) combinations are respectively even and odd under reflection
of the ladder, and hence carry transverse momenta \(k_y=0\) and \(k_y=\pi\).

For the stored real singlet pair field and real Hermitian exchange field, the
transpose relations are
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{ii'}^{\ell\ell'}=\alpha_{i'i}^{\ell'\ell},
 \qquad
 \beta_{\sigma,ii'}^{\ell\ell'}=\beta_{\sigma,i'i}^{\ell'\ell}.
 \label{eq:bond-transpose-symmetry}
\end{equation}
Consequently, a cross-leg odd component obeys
\begin{equation}
 q^{\times,-}_{i'i}=-q^{\times,-}_{ii'}.
 \label{eq:cross-leg-odd-reversal}
\end{equation}
It is therefore a legitimate diagonal-bond channel for \(i\ne i'\), but it
vanishes identically on a rung: \(q^{\times,-}_{ii}=0\).  A cross-leg rung pair
is necessarily leg-even, whereas the two oppositely oriented diagonals can form
either an even or an odd combination.  For \(\beta_\sigma\), an imaginary
antisymmetric rung amplitude would instead be a current/flux channel; complex
fields of that kind are outside the present real-field implementation.

Leg-reflection parity is not the same as the usual \(s\)- versus \(d\)-wave
form-factor label.  In particular, the two leg bonds of a \(d\)-wave pair field
have the same sign and are therefore leg-even; its \(d\)-wave character comes
from the opposite sign of the rung-bond component, that is, from a relation
between the same-leg and cross-leg blocks.

For each rung and spin, the Hartree update is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{pmatrix}\nu_{\sigma,i0}\\\nu_{\sigma,i1}\end{pmatrix}
 =K_G\left[
 \begin{pmatrix}n^\sigma_{i0}\\n^\sigma_{i1}\end{pmatrix}
 -\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}\right],
\label{eq:density-kernel}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
 K_G=
 \begin{cases}
  2g\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right),
    &G=\text{frustrated cubic},\\[2pt]
  6g\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right),
    &G=\text{unfrustrated cubic},\\[2pt]
  2g\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right),
    &G=\text{square}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The two-component Hartree field has the same even/odd leg-parity
classification as the same-leg bond block.  Table~\ref{tab:kernel-spectrum}
consolidates the spectra of every real field in
\(x=(\alpha,\beta_\downarrow,\beta_\uparrow,\nu_\downarrow,\nu_\uparrow)\).

\subsubsection{Kernel signs versus iteration signs}

A kernel eigenvalue \(k\) is not by itself the eigenvalue of the complete
linearized SCF map.  If \(\chi\ge0\) denotes the magnitude of the isolated
ladder's response in an unmixed eigenchannel, the source signs in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:linear-fields} give
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_{\alpha}=+k\chi_{\alpha},
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\beta}=-k\chi_{\beta},
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\nu}=-k\chi_{\nu}.
\label{eq:response-signs}
\end{equation}
The anomalous term is written as minus the pair field times its conjugate
operator, whereas the exchange and Hartree sources enter with plus signs.
Kernel signs must therefore be read with opposite response conventions in the
pair and normal one-body channels.

Equation~\eqref{eq:response-signs} is scalar bookkeeping for an isolated mode.
In general, the ladder response is a matrix: in particular, exchange and
Hartree perturbations with the same symmetries can mix.  The physical stability
numbers are therefore the eigenvalues of the full Jacobian
\(J=\partial f/\partial x\), not the entries in
Table~\ref{tab:kernel-spectrum}.  The table supplies the exact geometry part of
that Jacobian.

\paragraph{What is and is not diagonal.}
Let \(\bm r(x)\) denote all isolated-ladder correlators used to construct the
next field.  About a reference \(x_0\) (which must be a fixed point for an exact
stability analysis),
\begin{equation}
 \delta\bm r=\mathcal R\,\delta x,
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_{ab}=\left.\frac{\partial r_a}{\partial x_b}\right|_{x_0},
 \qquad
 J_G=K_G\mathcal R.
 \label{eq:full-response-composition}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathcal R\) includes the pair-versus-normal source signs in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:response-signs}; equivalently, those signs may be extracted and
the remaining susceptibility denoted by \(\chi\).  The tables above diagonalize
\(K_G\), not necessarily \(\mathcal R\).  Unless the two matrices commute, a
kernel eigenvector is not an eigenvector of \(J_G\).

Symmetry still imposes substantial block structure:
\begin{itemize}
 \item At a number-conserving reference, anomalous pair fields and normal
       Hartree/exchange fields do not mix to linear order.  They can mix after
       a paired state breaks \(U(1)\).
 \item At an unpolarized spin-symmetric reference, charge-like and spin-like
       normal perturbations form separate blocks.
 \item Exact leg reflection separates \(k_y=0\) and \(k_y=\pi\) blocks.
 \item Exact translation would separate longitudinal momenta \(q_x\).  The
       finite open ladder has no exact translation symmetry, so nearby standing
       waves and boundary-localized profiles can mix weakly or strongly.
\end{itemize}
Thus ``block diagonal'' is the appropriate expectation, not ``diagonal.''
Within one block, on-site, rung, leg-bond, longer-range, Hartree, and exchange
coordinates may mix whenever they share all exact quantum numbers.  In
particular, extended \(s\)- and \(d\)-wave pair templates are not distinct
irreducible representations of an anisotropic two-leg ladder: both are
leg-even, spin-singlet, \(q_x=0\) fields.  Their rung/leg relative sign is an
output to diagnose, not a symmetry prohibition against mixing.

\paragraph{Isolated-ladder intuition.}
Introduce sign-adjusted conjugate operators
\(\widetilde O_a=-\partial H/\partial x_a\), so every source is written as
\(-x_a\widetilde O_a\).  At zero temperature their static susceptibility has
the spectral form
\begin{equation}
 \chi_{ab}
 =2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{m>0}
 \frac{\langle0|\widetilde O_a|m\rangle
       \langle m|\widetilde O_b|0\rangle}
      {E_m-E_0}.
 \label{eq:spectral-susceptibility}
\end{equation}
An off-diagonal entry is nonzero when two different source operators couple the
ground state to the same excited states.  This is generic on a ladder.  A
leg-even density potential changes not only the density but also kinetic-energy
and exchange-bond expectation values; conversely, a hopping-like
\(\beta_\sigma\) perturbation redistributes charge.  Schematically,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{pmatrix}\delta n\\ \delta X\end{pmatrix}
 =-
 \begin{pmatrix}
   \chi_{nn} & \chi_{nX}\\
   \chi_{Xn} & \chi_{XX}
 \end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}\delta\nu\\ \delta\beta\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:normal-response-mixing}
\end{equation}
The same effect occurs in the pair block: rung hopping \(t_0\), longitudinal
hopping, and interactions allow a leg-bond pair source to induce a rung-pair
correlator and vice versa.  Reciprocity makes the properly normalized
susceptibility symmetric in the exact calculation, but the product
\(J_G=K_G\mathcal R\) need not be symmetric because the geometry kernel weights
the output coordinates differently.

\paragraph{A two-coordinate pair example.}
As a schematic example in the leg/rung basis, suppose the isolated ladder has
the sign structure
\begin{equation}
 \chi_{\mathrm{pair}}=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&-0.6\\-0.6&1.2\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:toy-pair-susceptibility}
\end{equation}
The negative off-diagonal response means that a positive leg source induces an
opposite-sign rung response, as expected for a \(d\)-like tendency.  For either
cubic geometry,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{J_{\mathrm{cubic}}}{g}
 =\begin{pmatrix}6&0\\0&4\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}1&-0.6\\-0.6&1.2\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}6&-3.6\\-2.4&4.8\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:toy-cubic-jacobian}
\end{equation}
This matrix is not diagonal.  Its eigenvalues are \(8.4g\) and \(2.4g\); the
leading right eigenvector is proportional to
\((1,-2/3)^{\mathsf T}\), not to either a pure leg field, a pure rung field, or
the equal-amplitude seed \((1,-1)^{\mathsf T}\).  It is the field pattern that
the complete raw map reproduces, multiplied by \(8.4g\).

For the same illustrative ladder response, the square kernel gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{J_{\mathrm{square}}}{g}
 =\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
  \chi_{\mathrm{pair}}
 =\begin{pmatrix}2&-1.2\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:toy-square-jacobian}
\end{equation}
The leg source can still induce a rung correlator through \(\chi_{\mathrm{pair}}\),
but the square kernel removes that output before it becomes the next rung
field.  The leading self-reproducing field direction is consequently pure leg
in this toy projection.

\paragraph{What the current Stage 2 calculation measures.}
Let the columns of \(Q\) be orthonormal candidate fields.  The finite-field
calculation measures every column of
\begin{equation}
 Z=\mathcal RQ,
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_Q=Q^{\mathsf T}Z,
 \qquad
 J_{G,Q}=Q^{\mathsf T}K_GZ.
 \label{eq:projected-stage2-response}
\end{equation}
Here \(Q^{\mathsf T}\) denotes projection using the implemented field metric.
It then diagonalizes the complete projected normal and pair blocks, rather than
treating the named candidates as independent scalar eigenmodes.  The present
bank contains nine normal Hartree directions and three independent pair
directions spanning the on-site, rung, and leg templates; the named extended
\(s\) and \(d\) templates are exact combinations inside that pair span.

This is a \emph{projected} Jacobian, not yet the literal full array-space
Jacobian.  In particular, the current normal input basis has no independent
\(\beta_\sigma\) source directions.  Exchange-field output is retained as a
\(\beta\)-fraction/leakage diagnostic.  If that leakage is appreciable, the
normal basis must be enlarged with exchange directions before its eigenvalues
can be called full normal-sector stability eigenvalues.  The same principle
applies to response outside the selected longitudinal modes or retained pair
templates.

For a linearized mode,
\begin{equation}
 \delta x_{m+1}=\lambda\,\delta x_m.
\end{equation}
A negative \(\lambda\) flips the mode's sign on every raw update.  If
\(|\lambda|<1\), this is merely a decaying oscillation into the normal fixed
point.  If \(|\lambda|>1\), the normal state is unstable along a
sign-alternating trajectory; nonlinear saturation can then produce two fields
that are exchanged by the raw map.  In the one-ladder formulation these two
iterations represent transverse sublattices \(A\) and \(B\).  In an explicit
two-sublattice formulation the same state is a fixed point of the doubled map,
whose linear factor is \(\lambda^2>0\).  This interpretation is physical only
when the transverse lattice supports the required bipartition; numerical
period two alone is not sufficient.

\subsubsection{Why the geometries promote \(d\)-wave pairing differently}

For the two leading bond classes, collect the leg-even nearest-neighbor
leg-bond correlator and the even rung-pair correlator into
\(\bm F_d=(F_{\parallel,+},F_{\perp,+})^{\mathsf T}\).  A
conventional ladder \(d\)-wave form factor has opposite signs on these two bond
classes, \(F_{\parallel,+}F_{\perp,+}<0\).  Although the kernel does not mix
different rung pairs, its action on this assembled form-factor vector is
\begin{equation}
 K^{(d)}_{\mathrm{frustrated}}
 =K^{(d)}_{\mathrm{unfrustrated}}
 =\begin{pmatrix}6g&0\\0&4g\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 K^{(d)}_{\mathrm{square}}
 =\begin{pmatrix}2g&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:d-wave-geometry-kernels}
\end{equation}
Both cubic geometries therefore provide exactly the same \emph{direct}
feedback to the \(d\)-wave sector.  They preserve the relative minus sign,
weight the leg-bond component by \(6g\), and weight the rung component by
\(4g\).  On the common leg-bond component this is three times the square
feedback.  More importantly, when the ladder's pair structure is rung-singlet
dominated, as in the strong-rung \(t_0>1\) regime of interest, the square kernel
discards the dominant component: it returns no rung-pair field at all.  In the
square connectivity, the two legs of a rung tunnel toward different neighboring
ladders, so the rung pair cannot make the same second-order pair hop into one
neighboring ladder.

Zero rung \emph{feedback} does not imply a zero rung-pair correlator.  The
isolated ladder can still convert a leg-bond source into a rung response through
off-diagonal entries of its pair susceptibility.  The missing square-kernel
entry means that this rung response is not fed back as a rung source on the next
SCF step.

At a common bare isolated-ladder expansion point, the response matrix
\(\chi_{\mathrm{pair}}\) is geometry independent, and
\begin{equation}
 J^{(d)}_G=K^{(d)}_G\chi_{\mathrm{pair}}.
 \label{eq:d-wave-jacobian}
\end{equation}
The displayed \(2\times2\) matrices summarize bond-class weights.  The full
susceptibility may also mix different longitudinal separations and the on-site
pair component, but the equality of the two cubic kernels persists throughout
the leg-even pair block.
Hence the two cubic geometries have identical bare Jacobians within the
leg-even \(d\)-wave subspace; any difference between their final \(d\)-wave
solutions must enter indirectly through competing normal channels,
geometry-dependent normal-state dressing, or nonlinear selection.
Equation~\eqref{eq:d-wave-geometry-kernels} also shows
that there is generally no single scalar ``\(d\)-wave kernel eigenvalue'': the
leg and rung coordinates have different weights, and the full eigenvector and
eigenvalue come from \(K^{(d)}_G\chi_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  The shorthand
\(\lambda=k\chi\) is exact only for an unmixed eigenmode.

The two cubic geometries separate most clearly in the leg-odd sectors.  The
same-leg/local \(k_y=\pi\) kernel is \(-6g\) in the unfrustrated geometry but
only \(+2g\) in the frustrated geometry: the magnitude is reduced by a factor
of three and the sign is reversed.  For a leg-odd pair field this is a competing
pair channel, not the conventional leg-even \(d\)-wave channel.  For a leg-odd
Hartree or exchange field, the extra minus response sign in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:response-signs} gives the opposite transverse interpretation:
the unfrustrated mode is transversely in phase, whereas the weaker frustrated
mode wants to alternate between neighboring ladders.  This leg-odd sector is
the natural leg-staggered SDW/CDW competitor.

There is a second, distinct frustration effect in the leg-even normal sector.
Both cubic kernels have \(k=+6g\), so the normal response gives
\(\lambda<0\) and favors an \(A/B\) alternation between neighboring ladders.
The bipartite unfrustrated transverse lattice can realize that pattern; the
non-bipartite frustrated lattice cannot satisfy it on every bond.  Thus
``leg-odd'' (\(k_y=\pi\) within one ladder) and ``transversely staggered''
(negative SCF \(\lambda\)) are independent labels and should not be conflated.
More generally, no negative-\(\lambda\) channel has a globally consistent
two-sublattice realization on the frustrated connectivity; a numerical
period-two orbit there is not automatically a physical state.

The remaining odd pairing channel is also informative but is not part of a
uniform rung \(d\)-wave form factor.  The diagonal-only cross-leg odd mode has
kernel eigenvalues \(+4g\), \(-4g\), and \(0\) in the frustrated,
unfrustrated, and square geometries, respectively.  It vanishes for a rung pair
by Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-leg-odd-reversal}.

\paragraph{Direct comparison with CDW and SDW.}
Define the charge- and spin-like Hartree sources and responses by
\begin{equation}
 \nu_c=\frac{\nu_\uparrow+\nu_\downarrow}{\sqrt{2}},
 \qquad
 \nu_s=\frac{\nu_\uparrow-\nu_\downarrow}{\sqrt{2}},
 \qquad
 \chi_c\ne\chi_s\quad\text{in general}.
 \label{eq:charge-spin-fields}
\end{equation}
The geometry kernel is spin blind, so CDW and SDW fields with the same leg
parity have the same \(k\).  Their physical responses differ through the
longitudinal wavevector and the isolated-ladder susceptibilities \(\chi_c\) and
\(\chi_s\).  With hole density \(\delta=1-n_\star\), the principal candidates
used in this project are a leg-even CDW near
\(q_x\simeq2\pi\delta\) (the \(4k_F\) wavevector modulo \(2\pi\)) and a
leg-odd SDW near \(q_x\simeq\pi(1-\delta)\).  Table~\ref{tab:dwave-cdw-sdw}
compares these with uniform \(d\)-wave pairing.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXccc@{}}
\toprule
Tendency / component & Field and ladder sector
& Frustrated cubic & Unfrustrated cubic & Square \\
\midrule
\(d\)-wave, leg bond
& \(\alpha_{\parallel,+}\), \(q_x=0\), \(k_y=0\)
& \(+6g\) & \(+6g\) & \(+2g\) \\
\(d\)-wave, rung bond
& \(\alpha_{\perp,+}\), \(q_x=0\), cross-leg even

\label{tab:dwave-cdw-sdw}
\end{table}

The table makes the relative mechanisms transparent:
\begin{itemize}
 \item In the frustrated cubic geometry, \(d\)-wave feedback remains strong and
       positive.  The leading leg-even CDW factor has the same magnitude
       \(6g\), but its negative sign calls for a transverse two-sublattice
       pattern that is incompatible with the non-bipartite connectivity.  The
       leg-odd SDW factor is both three times weaker than in the unfrustrated
       cubic geometry and negative, so its simple transverse alternation is
       frustrated as well.
 \item In the unfrustrated cubic geometry, the \(d\)-wave leg component, the
       charge-even CDW, and the spin-odd SDW all carry geometry factors of
       magnitude \(6g\).  The CDW factor is negative and its transverse
       \(A/B\) pattern is allowed; the SDW factor is positive and is in phase
       across ladders.  Pairing therefore receives no comparable relief from
       its main normal competitors, and the winner is controlled primarily by
       \(\chi_{\mathrm{pair}}\), \(\chi_c\), and \(\chi_s\).
 \item In the square geometry, the common leg scale is only \(2g\), so both
       pairing and normal tendencies are weakened relative to cubic.  The
       decisive asymmetry is that CDW and SDW feedback remains nonzero and can
       use the bipartite transverse pattern where needed, whereas the dominant
       rung component of \(d\)-wave pairing receives exactly zero direct
       feedback.
\end{itemize}

These are Hartree-channel comparisons.  A complete CDW or SDW eigenmode may
also contain the charge- or spin-like combination of \(\beta_\sigma\).  Because
exchange and Hartree perturbations can mix, the quantitative comparison is
between the spectral radii of the full pair, charge, and spin Jacobian blocks,
not between isolated entries of Table~\ref{tab:dwave-cdw-sdw}.  Geometry fixes
the feedback weights and transverse sign constraints; the ladder response
decides how strongly each longitudinal order parameter exploits them.

When the isolated ladder is spin gapped, the gap provides an additional,
geometry-independent suppression of the low-energy SDW response.  It can favor
singlet pairing relative to SDW, but it does not distinguish the three
transverse geometries; that distinction still comes from the kernels above.
The pairing--CDW competition can therefore remain the closer one.  Equal-time
correlation amplitudes or decay exponents are useful diagnostics but cannot be
substituted for the static susceptibilities in the Jacobian comparison.

The resulting kernel-level expectation is that
\texttt{cubic\_frustrated} has the strongest net tendency to stabilize
\(d\)-wave order: it retains the same \(6g/4g\) pair feedback as
\texttt{cubic\_unfrustrated} while weakening or geometrically frustrating key
normal competitors.  The unfrustrated cubic geometry comes next, and the square
geometry is least favorable because it has only \(2g\) leg feedback and no
rung feedback.  This is a mechanism and ranking hypothesis, not by itself a
phase-boundary result.  A dressed susceptibility can depend indirectly on
geometry, and a non-bipartite array may support multi-sublattice or complex
orders outside the present real one-/two-sublattice description.  Final claims
must use the measured full Jacobian together with converged variational-energy
comparisons.

\section{MPS ground-state solve and density targeting}

\subsection{Finite-system DMRG}

For fixed \(x_k\) and \(\mu\), the ground state of \(\Heff\) is approximated by
finite-system DMRG using ITensorMPS.  A cold start uses a randomized product
state with particle count nearest \(2Ln_\star\); subsequent MF and chemical
potential solves warm-start from the previous MPS.  The observer records each
sweep energy, maximum discarded weight, and maximum link dimension.  It may
terminate only after reaching the final configured bond dimension with zero
noise and satisfying the sweep-energy tolerance, or when the wall-time
deadline is reached.

Production GPU calculations use dense tensors in Float64, with \(S_z\) and
fermion-parity quantum-number block structure disabled for performance.  This
changes the tensor representation, not the Hamiltonian.  Separate fixed-sector
gap calculations use QN-conserving CPU MPS states.

\subsection{Safeguarded chemical-potential search}

Each MF update contains an inner solve of
\begin{equation}
  h(\mu)=n(\mu;x_k)-n_\star=0.
\end{equation}
The algorithm first evaluates the previous \(\mu\).  If a positive carried
slope \(\kappa\simeq\dd n/\dd\mu\) is available, it tries a bounded Newton
predictor.  Otherwise, or if that predictor does not bracket the target, it
expands a bracket geometrically in the density-correct direction.  Within a
bracket it uses a safeguarded secant step, rejecting points within ten percent
of either endpoint in favor of bisection.  Every trial reuses the nearest MPS
and a small warm-start noise schedule.  The best-density state is returned on
an iteration limit, but it cannot pass final acceptance unless the configured
density gate is met.

\section{Self-consistency, mixing, and recurrence}
\label{sec:scf}

One outer iteration is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item solve \(\Heff[x_k,\mu_k]\) by density-targeted DMRG;
  \item measure \(y_k=f(x_k)\) and the associated correlators;
  \item evaluate field residuals and the variational-energy identities;
  \item classify the recent history as a fixed point, physical orbit,
        candidate, or incomplete numerical trajectory; and
  \item if nonterminal, choose \(x_{k+1}\) by the raw map or an accelerator.
\end{enumerate}

The field residuals use the complete stored field vector,
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_{\infty}=\max_j|(y_k-x_k)_j|,\qquad
 \epsilon_2=\frac{\|y_k-x_k\|_2}
 {\max(\|y_k\|_2,\|x_k\|_2,\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}})}.
\label{eq:field-residuals}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Unmixed probe and physical orbits}

The production-shaped workflow begins with complete raw updates
\(x_{k+1}=f(x_k)\), before damping or Anderson acceleration.  A period-\(q\)
recurrence requires every phase to close over the configured number of repeat
links.  With three repeat links, a period-two test needs eight contiguous raw
records.  Acceptance additionally requires:
\begin{itemize}
  \item raw-map closure \(x_k\simeq y_{k-1}\) for every stored link;
  \item target density and per-phase energy recurrence;
  \item the Hamiltonian and effective-eigenvalue consistency checks in
        Sec.~\ref{sec:variational}; and
  \item explicit inclusion of \(q\) in the configured accepted-period list.
\end{itemize}
For \(q=2\), successive step vectors must reverse direction: the worst recent
cosine must be below its negative threshold, and
\(\|y_k-y_{k-2}\|/\|y_k-y_{k-1}\|\) must remain below its configured bound.
This rejects a slowly drifting trajectory that happens to be close at lag two.

A mixer-dependent recurrence is only a candidate.  It is archived, mixer
history is cleared, and a fresh raw-map probe is required before it may be
accepted.  Orbit phases are stored separately and never replaced by their
field average.  The physical period-two construction follows the logic of
Ref.~\cite{bollmark2025}; naming an accepted orbit ``CDW'' still requires a
nonzero bulk density contrast, the expected spatial pattern, and stability
with \(L\), bond dimension, and bulk window.  The stored contrast is the mean
absolute density difference between neighboring orbit phases over a centered
bulk fraction of the ladder (one half by default).

\subsection{Fixed-point gate and slow modes}

A period-one solution must satisfy both the raw residual gate in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:field-residuals} and a slow-mode extrapolation.  For residual
vectors \(r_k=y_k-x_k\),
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_k=\frac{r_k\cdot r_{k-1}}{\|r_{k-1}\|_2^2}.
\end{equation}
When consecutive residuals are sufficiently parallel, the gated residual is
inflated by
\begin{equation}
  s_k=\max\left(1,\frac{1}{1-\lambda_k}\right).
\end{equation}
If \(\lambda_k\ge1\), the factor is infinite.  This prevents a nearly marginal
mode from being accepted merely because its instantaneous residual crosses a
local threshold.  The recent stability window must also pass the density gate;
the latest target-density-corrected energy change and the current energy
identities must pass their respective gates.

\subsection{Linear and Anderson acceleration}

If the initial raw probe finds no accepted solution, the code uses adaptive
linear or Anderson mixing.  Linear mixing is
\begin{equation}
 x_{k+1}=x_k+\eta_k\bigl[f(x_k)-x_k\bigr].
\end{equation}
For Anderson mixing, recent residuals \(r_j=f_j-x_j\) are combined with
coefficients \(c_j\), constrained by \(\sum_jc_j=1\), by solving the regularized
Gram/KKT system.  The update is
\begin{equation}
 x_{k+1}=(1-\eta_k)\sum_jc_jx_j+\eta_k\sum_jc_jf_j.
\end{equation}
The damping is halved after substantial residual growth and increased
conservatively after improvement, within configured minimum and maximum
bounds.  Mixing accelerates a fixed-point search; it does not certify a
physical orbit.

Independent branches deliberately sample multiple basins.  The currently
implemented seed families are summarized below.

\section{Zero-temperature variational functional}
\label{sec:variational}

The DMRG eigenvalue \(E_{\mathrm{eig}}\) of \(\Heff\) is not directly
comparable between MF branches: it includes \(-\mu N\) and only the linearized
transverse terms.  Let \(L_{\mathrm{lin}}=\mean{H_{\mathrm{lin}}}\).  Channel
by channel,
\begin{align}
 L_{\mathrm{pair}}&=-2\sum_a\alpha_a F_a,
 &E_{\perp,\mathrm{pair}}&=\tfrac12L_{\mathrm{pair}},\\
 L_{\mathrm{ex}}&=\sum_a\beta_a X_a,
 &E_{\perp,\mathrm{ex}}&=\tfrac12L_{\mathrm{ex}},\\
 L_{\mathrm{dens}}&=\sum_a\nu_a n_a,
 &E_{\perp,\mathrm{dens}}&=\tfrac12\sum_a\nu_a(n_a-\tfrac12).
\end{align}
The applied partner fields are used in these contractions.  At a fixed point
they equal the outgoing fields; on an orbit they belong to the preceding
phase and must remain distinct.

With \(E_\perp\) the sum of the three quadratic MF contributions, the
double-counting constant is
\begin{equation}
 C_{\mathrm{dc}}=E_\perp-L_{\mathrm{lin}}.
\end{equation}
The reconstructed canonical functional is
 \begin{equation}
 E_{\mathrm{var}}^{\mathrm{rec}}
 =\mean{\Heff}+\mu\mean N+C_{\mathrm{dc}},
\end{equation}
and the independently evaluated direct form is
\begin{equation}
 E_{\mathrm{var}}^{\mathrm{dir}}=\mean{\Hlad}+E_\perp.
\label{eq:direct-functional}
\end{equation}
The code stores Eq.~\eqref{eq:direct-functional} as the canonical variational
energy and requires agreement with the reconstructed form.  It also checks
\begin{equation}
 \mean{\Hlad}=\mean{\Heff}+\mu\mean N-L_{\mathrm{lin}}
\label{eq:hamiltonian-identity}
\end{equation}
and separately compares \(E_{\mathrm{eig}}\) with \(\mean{\Heff}\).

Small residual density errors are placed on the common target-\(N\) tangent
plane using
\begin{equation}
 E_{N_\star}=E_{\mathrm{var}}^{\mathrm{dir}}
              +\mu\bigl(N_\star-\mean N\bigr).
\label{eq:density-correction}
\end{equation}
This leading correction is used for energy-stability gates and branch ranking;
it is not a substitute for tighter density convergence.  For a physical
orbit, canonical and corrected energies are computed phase by phase with the
correct partner field, then averaged arithmetically.

Only accepted fixed points or validated unmixed orbits may be ranked.  The
model, geometry, numerical settings, implementation, and \(\Ep\)-registry
fingerprints must match.  Cross-geometry energies are not ranked because the
geometries define different effective Hamiltonians, and a possible
field-independent perturbative offset has not been restored.

\section{Observables and validation diagnostics}
\label{sec:diagnostics}

\subsection{Structure factors and \(K_\rho\)}

For \(O_i=n_i\) or \(S_i^z\), the connected structure factor is
\begin{equation}
 S_O(q_x,q_y)=\frac{1}{2L}\sum_{a,b}
 e^{\mathrm{i}[q_x(i_a-i_b)+q_y(\ell_a-\ell_b)]}
 \left(\mean{O_aO_b}-\mean{O_a}\mean{O_b}\right),
\end{equation}
with \(q_x=2\pi m/L\) and \(q_y\in\{0,\pi\}\).  The dominant charge and spin
wavevectors are stored.  The reference \(q_x=\pi n\) is a tracking diagnostic,
not a universal two-leg-ladder phase criterion.

The first up to three nonzero \(q_y=0\) charge modes are fit through the origin
to obtain \(s=\dd S_c/\dd q\).  Both normalizations are reported,
\begin{equation}
 K_{\rho,\mathrm{site}}=\pi s,
 \qquad K_{\rho,\mathrm{rung}}=2\pi s.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Entanglement and pair correlations}

For every MPS bond, Schmidt probabilities \(p_a\) give
\begin{equation}
 S=-\sum_ap_a\log p_a,
 \qquad S_2=-\log\sum_ap_a^2.
\end{equation}
The central-charge diagnostic uses inter-rung cuts away from the open edges and
fits
\begin{equation}
 S(\ell)=\frac{c}{6}\log\left[\frac{2L}{\pi}
              \sin\left(\frac{\pi\ell}{L}\right)\right]+s_0.
\end{equation}
The fit window, number of points, and \(R^2\) are stored.  Finite-size,
finite-entanglement, and parity effects can make this estimate unreliable.

Optional full pair diagnostics use the unnormalized singlet operator
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{ab}=c_{a\uparrow}c_{b\downarrow}
             -c_{a\downarrow}c_{b\uparrow}
\end{equation}
and retain sign-resolved matrices for rung, leg-0, and leg-1 bonds.  Spatial
decay and sign structure must be reported; a large local value or finite-MPS
pair field alone does not establish superconducting long-range order.

\subsection{Fixed-sector gaps}

Separate QN-conserving DMRG calculations evaluate
\begin{align}
 \Delta_s &= E(N,2)-E(N,0),\\
 \Delta_c &= \tfrac12\left[E(N+2,0)+E(N-2,0)-2E(N,0)\right],\\
 \Ep^{(h)} &= E(N-2,0)+E(N,0)-2E(N-1,1),\\
 \Ep^{(p)} &= E(N+2,0)+E(N,0)-2E(N+1,1),
\end{align}
where the second argument is \(2S_z\).  The present implementation assumes an
even reference \(N\) and an \(S_z=0\) reference sector.  Per-sector sweep
energies, discarded weights, and maximum link dimensions are stored as
convergence evidence.

\section{Artifacts, provenance, and branch selection}

Each HDF5 state records the complete configuration, applied and measured field
history, correlators and energy decomposition, DMRG sweep evidence, model and
numerical fingerprints, implementation and registry hashes, software/device
metadata, random seed, branch labels, and hash-pinned parent or resume lineage.
Rolling latest/best checkpoints are replaceable; final states and orbit
artifacts are immutable.

Full production states, including MPS objects, live in scratch storage.
Compact stateless mirrors omit MPS tensors but retain the analysis and
selection data plus hashes and the full-artifact location.  A compact state may
be plotted, compared, or used for field-only inheritance, but cannot be used as
an MPS restart.

The comparison tool admits only accepted period-one states or explicitly
enabled, raw-map-validated orbits with matching fingerprints.  For scans, keep
independent, forward, reverse, and metastable continuations as distinct
lineages rather than overwriting a higher-energy basin.
